## Identity and audience

**Ad title:** LOCALHOST: LEVEL UP

**Product name:** Local Dev Manager

**Tagline:** Your projects. One dashboard. More building.

Audience: casual developers, weekend builders, students, and people juggling small websites and side projects. Show the satisfying moment when scattered projects become an organized, runnable workspace. Explain benefits in ordinary language; keep implementation details out of the ad.

Target: approximately 60 seconds, English narration, 1920 x 1080 at 30 FPS. Design the main action so a separate portrait edit can crop or recompose it; do not shrink the entire desktop recording into an unreadable vertical frame.

## Production direction

### Voice-over

Friendly, energetic conversational English. Slight amused pause on the opening port question; confident delivery on Start; warm finish. Use a generic synthetic voice or an authorized original recording. Avoid announcer shouting. Roughly 125-140 words per minute with breathing room; adjust shots to measured speech rather than forcing audio to fit. Pronounce localhost naturally as local host.

### Subtitles

Burn in the exact spoken narration and export an SRT. Use high-contrast white text on a dark translucent backing, up to two lines, outside important app controls. Aim for short phrase groups with natural pauses and approximately 32-42 characters per line. Generate timing from the actual voice audio. Highlight a key word in mint occasionally; do not call the captions word-synchronized unless actual word timing is produced. Headlines add emphasis and do not replace subtitles. Keep final text legible on a phone.

### Animation and creative hovers

Dark navy framing, mint and violet accents, large kinetic typography, subtle grid parallax, and focused camera moves. Let the real UI occupy most of each feature shot. Alternate short energetic transitions with readable holds; avoid continuous spinning or flashing.

Creative hover effects are **video overlays**, not new product features: cursor halo, restrained magnetic-looking approach, small button enlargement, card lift, and click ripples. Keep them anchored to the actual controls. Show real actions and recorded states; preserve normal interface text. Use continuous cursor movement without teleports. Hold the important hover briefly before a click and clear decorative overlays when they obstruct a control.

### Music and sound effects

Use an original or appropriately licensed instrumental electronic groove around 112 BPM: warm bass, crisp percussion, playful synth arpeggio, no vocals. Begin filtered, open up at the title, thin the arrangement beneath the access-review sentence, and resolve on the final card. Duck the music beneath speech. Add restrained clicks, taps, paper flicks, whooshes, readiness chime, theme-toggle tick, and a closing tone. Attach each effect to a visible event; keep voice clearly dominant. Do not use copyrighted commercial tracks without rights.

### Capture and claims

Use the actual Electron app and fictional isolated fixture projects, following the existing montage capture/privacy approach. Redact paths for presentation. Never fabricate statuses, logs, or startup performance. A verified Running server does not prove every app route works. Mark condensed startup waits.

The preview scene uses the existing confirmation-only demonstration; it does not start a public tunnel. Link graphics are editorial illustrations and must not impersonate a real active URL. Actual live public-preview footage requires a separately authorized controlled capture. Temporary previews require the local app/server and internet to stay available; they are not permanent hosting.

The current product is a Windows source-build preview. Do not claim a ready installer, macOS/Linux support, execution support for every detected package manager/framework, permanent deployment, AI features, or licensed open-source availability. npm projects need an existing dev script and installed project dependencies. The manager itself needs its documented setup; plain HTML support does not mean the manager needs no runtime.
